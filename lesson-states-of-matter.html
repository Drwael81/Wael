Core Concept

What is Matter?

Definition
Everything that has mass and occupies space.
Classified according to
Its physical state, into: solid, liquid, and gaseous.
Its mass
Is a constant quantity that does not change from one place to another.
❓ All of the following are objects that have the properties of matter, except: copper – music – ruler – computer
✅ Answer: Music — copper, a ruler and a computer are materials that have mass and occupy space, while music is sound energy, not matter.
Property 1

Shape and Fluidity

When different materials are transferred from one container to another, it can be noticed that some change shape and some don't.

Solid — e.g. wood, iron
Neither flows nor changes shape
Its shape is fixed and definite; it does not take the shape of the container.
Liquid — e.g. water, oil
Flows and changes shape
Takes the shape of the container that holds it.
Gas — e.g. air, oxygen
Flows and changes shape
Takes the shape of the container that holds it.
We conclude Liquids and gases share the property of fluidity, so they are grouped together as fluids (materials that flow and take the shape of their container). A solid's shape does not change when transferred from place to place.

⚠️ Take care: Liquids differ in fluidity according to their viscosity. When fluids flow from one container to another, the volume of a liquid stays constant, while the volume of a gas changes.
Property 2 · Interactive

Volume and Compressibility

Three identical syringes, sealed with plugs, hold equal volumes of different substances. Press each plunger and watch what happens.

Solid — fine sand
Volume: 100%
Cannot be compressed — volume stays constant.
Liquid — water
Volume: 100%
Cannot be compressed — volume stays constant.
Gas — air
Volume: 100%
Can be compressed — volume decreases.
We conclude Solids and liquids have a fixed (definite) volume and are both incompressible. Gases do not have a definite volume and are compressible.

📌 Compressibility: the possibility of decreasing the volume of gases by increasing the pressure exerted on them.
❓ A closed bottle is squeezed and its shape deforms. What happened to the air inside it? (Click the bottle to squeeze it)
Tap the bottle to squeeze it →
Since the bottle is closed: there is no air flow, no diffusion, and no increase in air volume.
✅ Therefore, the deformation of the bottle shows that the air inside it was compressed due to the pressure applied on the bottle.
Property 3 · Interactive

Diffusibility

Diffusion: the movement of particles of matter from a region of high concentration to a region of low concentration.

Experiment: Ammonia (NH₃) meets Hydrogen Chloride (HCl)

An open-ended tube has cotton (1) wetted with HCl acid on one end, releasing hydrogen chloride gas, and cotton (2) wetted with ammonia solution on the other end, releasing ammonia gas. Where the two gases meet, a white cloud forms.

⚠️ Take care:
• The speed of diffusion depends on the temperature of the medium — it increases as temperature increases.
• The speed of diffusion of gas molecules increases as their mass decreases.
❓ Based on the tube experiment above:
1) Which gas diffuses faster? Ammonia gas diffuses faster than hydrogen chloride gas, because the white cloud forms nearer to cotton (1), wetted with HCl acid — meaning ammonia molecules travelled a longer distance in the same time.

2) Which gas molecule has greater mass? The hydrogen chloride molecule has greater mass, because its speed of diffusion is lower (mass and diffusion speed are inversely related).

Quick Recap

Key Takeaways

Fluids = liquids + gases (they flow)
Solids & liquids: fixed volume, incompressible
Gases only: compressible
Diffusion speed: solid < liquid < gas
Molecule mass ↑ ⇒ diffusion speed ↓
Temperature ↑ ⇒ diffusion speed ↑
