The Assumptions of the Particle Theory of Matter

Any matter is composed of very small units called particles. The particle theory successfully explains the behaviour and properties of matter.

1

Matter is composed of very small particles that are not visible to the naked eye.

2

The particles of a single type of matter (the same substance) are identical, but they differ from one substance to another.

3

The particles of any substance store potential energy due to the existence of attraction forces between them.

4

There are spaces between the particles of any substance, called interparticle spaces, which vary depending on the physical state of the substance.

5

The particles of any substance have kinetic energy because they are in a state of continuous motion in straight lines.

6

The way of movement and speed of the particles differ, based on the physical state of the substance.

Three States of Matter

🧊 Solid

Cause: Attraction forces are very strong, interparticle spaces are very small.
Effect: Definite shape and volume, incompressible, very slow diffusion.

Particles vibrate in their fixed position only.

💧 Liquid

Cause: Attraction forces are relatively weak, interparticle spaces are relatively large.
Effect: No definite shape (flows), definite volume, incompressible, average diffusion.

Particles move more freely within the container.

💨 Gas

Cause: Attraction forces are very weak, interparticle spaces are very large.
Effect: No definite shape or volume, compressible, very fast diffusion.

Particles move randomly and completely freely in all directions.

🌀 Brownian Motion

In 1827, scientist Robert Brown discovered that pollen grains suspended in water are in a state of continuous random motion in all directions.

Definition: Brownian motion is the random motion of relatively large particles suspended in a fluid, in all directions, as a result of their continuous collisions with the molecules of the fluid.

⚡ The 4th State: Plasma

When gases are heated to extremely high temperatures (thousands or even millions of °C), they become ionized — particles break apart into positively charged ions and negatively charged free electrons.

🔹 Characteristics: High electrical conductivity.

🔹 Examples: Sun, stars, lightning, polar aurora (Aurora borealis).

🔹 Application: Plasma air conditioners that purify air from pollutants and germs.

❓ Explain Based on the Particle Theory

🪨 Difficulty of Breaking a Piece of Rock

Because the forces of attraction between particles in solid rock are very strong, and the interparticle spaces are very small, which makes the particles tightly bound together.

🥄 Dissolving Table Salt in Water

Because the forces of attraction between particles of liquid water are relatively weak, and the interparticle spaces are relatively large, so they can be occupied by table salt particles.

🕯️ Diffusion of Candle Smoke in the Air

Because the forces of attraction between air particles are very weak, and the interparticle spaces between them are very large, so smoke particles diffuse in it.
